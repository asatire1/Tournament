What is Padel Americano? Rules, Format & How to Play

14 Feb 2025 Americano

If you have ever turned up to a social padel session and been told "we're playing Americano", you are not alone in wondering what that means. Padel Americano is the most popular social tournament format in the padel world, and for good reason. It is designed to maximise fun, keep everyone playing, and ensure that every player on court gets a fair chance to compete -- regardless of their level.

Unlike a traditional knockout bracket where you are paired with one partner for the entire event, Americano rotates your partner every round. That means you will play with and against different people throughout the session. At the end, the player with the highest individual score wins. It is social, it is competitive, and it is incredibly easy to organise.

Whether you are a club organiser looking for a format that works every week, or a group of friends wanting a structured way to play, Americano is the answer. In this guide we will walk through exactly how it works, the rules, how scoring is handled, how many players you need, and how to set one up yourself.

How Americano Works

The core idea behind Americano is simple: pre-set fixtures with rotating partners. Before the tournament begins, every round is planned out in advance. Each round assigns players into pairs and places them on a court. After the round is played, the pairs change, and new fixtures are generated for the next round.

The fixture schedule ensures that every player plays the same number of matches over the course of the tournament. Ideally, each player partners with every other player at least once and faces every other player at least once on the opposite side of the net. This balanced structure is what makes Americano feel fair and social at the same time.

Because partnerships rotate, no single player is tied to a stronger or weaker partner for the whole event. Over multiple rounds the randomness evens out, and the player who performs most consistently across all their matches rises to the top of the leaderboard.

The Rules of Padel Americano

Americano follows the standard rules of padel, but with a specific tournament structure layered on top. Here are the key rules that define the format:

  • 4 players per court per round. Every match is a doubles match, played two against two, just like regular padel.
  • Partners change every round. The fixture list dictates who plays with whom. You will rarely, if ever, play with the same partner twice in a row.
  • Matches are played to a fixed number of points. Rather than traditional sets and games, Americano matches are typically played to 16, 24, or 32 points. The total is split between the two teams. For example, in a match to 32 points, if one team wins 20–12, each player on the winning side earns 20 points and each player on the losing side earns 12. Every point matters.
  • Scoring is individual, not team-based. Although you play as a pair in each round, your points are tallied against your own name. At the end of the tournament you are ranked individually.
  • Service follows standard padel rules. The serving team alternates servers as in regular padel, and standard padel rules for lets, faults, and walls all apply.
  • All players play the same number of rounds. Nobody sits out. This is one of the biggest advantages over bracket-style tournaments, where losers are eliminated early.

Why Players Love Americano

Ask any padel player what their favourite social format is and most will say Americano. Here is why it resonates so strongly with the padel community:

  • Social mixing. Because partners rotate every round, you get to play with everyone in the group. It is the fastest way to get to know new players and build connections within a club or social circle.
  • Fair for all levels. Since everyone partners with everyone, stronger players are balanced out by also partnering with weaker players. Over the course of the event, skill differences average out more fairly than in fixed-pair formats.
  • No waiting around. Every player is assigned to a match in every round. There are no byes, no sitting on the sideline watching. From the first round to the last, you are on court.
  • Quick to set up. You do not need a complex bracket, seedings, or a draw ceremony. With a tool like UberPadel, you just enter player names, choose the number of rounds, and the fixtures are generated automatically.
  • Every point counts. Because you keep every point you score (win or lose), there is always something to play for. Even if you are losing a match, getting extra points improves your individual standing.

How Scoring Works in Americano

Scoring in Americano is refreshingly straightforward. Each match is played to a predetermined total -- commonly 32 points. The total is always distributed between the two teams. If Team A beats Team B with a score of 19–13, then each player on Team A gets 19 points added to their personal tally, and each player on Team B gets 13 points.

After all rounds are completed, every player's points from every round are added up. The player with the highest cumulative total wins the tournament. In the event of a tie, most organisers use head-to-head results or point difference as a tiebreaker.

This system is elegant because it rewards consistency. You cannot coast on one big win -- you need to perform well across every single match. It also means that even the last round is meaningful, since a few extra points could be the difference between first and second place.

How Many Players Do You Need?

Americano is remarkably flexible when it comes to group size. Here is a general guide:

  • 4 players (1 court): The minimum. Each round, all four players are on the same court. Partners rotate so you play with each person at least once. A quick session, perfect for a casual hit.
  • 8 players (2 courts): The sweet spot. Two courts running simultaneously means every round has all eight players in action. Enough rounds to play with and against everyone, typically 7 rounds for a full rotation.
  • 12 players (3 courts): A great size for club events. With three courts, you can run a complete Americano in around 11 rounds, though many organisers play fewer rounds to keep the session to 2–2.5 hours.
  • 16 players (4 courts): The practical upper limit for a standard Americano. With four courts and 16 players, the fixture calculation becomes more complex, but tools like UberPadel handle it automatically.

For groups larger than 16, you can either split into two parallel Americano groups or consider switching to a Mexicano format, which uses dynamic matchmaking based on the leaderboard rather than pre-set fixtures.

How to Set Up an Americano Tournament

Setting up an Americano used to involve spreadsheets, printed fixture tables, and a lot of mental arithmetic. Today, it takes less than two minutes with the right tool. Here is the process:

  1. Gather your players. Confirm how many people are coming and how many courts you have available. Americano works best when the number of players is a multiple of four.
  2. Choose your match format. Decide whether each match will be played to 16, 24, or 32 points. Shorter matches (16 points) work well for larger groups where you want more rounds in less time. Longer matches (32 points) suit smaller groups who want each round to feel substantial.
  3. Generate fixtures. This is where UberPadel comes in. Enter your player names, select the number of courts and points per match, and the system generates a balanced fixture schedule instantly. No manual work required.
  4. Play the rounds. Call out the fixtures for each round, let the matches play out, and enter the scores as they come in. UberPadel updates the leaderboard in real time.
  5. Crown the winner. After the final round, the player at the top of the leaderboard wins. Share the results, congratulate the champion, and plan the next one.

The beauty of Americano is that it runs itself once the fixtures are set. There is no need for a referee, no complicated bracket management, and no arguments about who plays next. Everyone knows their matches, the scores are tracked live, and the leaderboard speaks for itself.

Whether you are running a weekly club night or a one-off social event, Americano delivers the perfect blend of competition and community. It is the format that keeps players coming back week after week -- and now you know exactly how it works.
