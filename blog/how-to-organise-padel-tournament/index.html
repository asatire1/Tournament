How to Organise a Padel Tournament: Step-by-Step Guide

14 Feb 2025 Guide

Introduction

Running a padel tournament does not have to be complicated. Whether you are organising a casual session with friends or a bigger event at your club, the process is straightforward if you follow a clear plan. Pick the right format, gather your players, book courts, and let UberPadel handle fixtures and scoring for you.

Step 1: Decide on the Format

The format you choose determines how many players you need, how matches are drawn, and how long the tournament will last. Here are the four main formats supported by UberPadel:

Americano

Padel Americano is the most popular social format. Every player partners with a different teammate each round. Ideal for 4 to 24 players, with individual scores tracked on a single leaderboard.

Mexicano

Mexicano uses dynamic matchmaking. After each round, the system pairs players based on current standings so closely ranked players face each other. Great when skill levels vary within the group.

Team Tournament

Players are locked into fixed pairs for the entire event in a round-robin format. Best for groups with established partnerships who want consistent team play.

Mix Tournament

The Mix format is designed for large groups of 20 to 28 players. Players are divided into skill tiers and the system generates 15 rounds of balanced fixtures. It is the best option for club events where you need to keep a big group engaged.

Step 2: Gather Your Players

Once you have picked a format, the next step is confirming your player list. The number of players you need depends on the format:

  • Americano: 4 to 24 players (must be divisible by 4 for even court allocation).
  • Mexicano: 4 to 24 players, same divisibility requirement.
  • Team Tournament: 3 to 12 teams (6 to 24 players in fixed pairs).
  • Mix Tournament: 20 to 28 players, ideally grouped by self-assessed skill level.

Collect player names in advance. For the Mix format, also ask each player for a skill level so the system can create balanced tiers. A shared message or sign-up sheet works perfectly.

Step 3: Book Your Courts

Court availability is often the biggest constraint, so plan this early. A good rule of thumb is one court for every four players. Here is what each format typically requires:

  • Americano (8 players): 2 courts.
  • Americano (16 players): 4 courts.
  • Mexicano (12 players): 3 courts.
  • Team Tournament (8 teams): 4 courts running simultaneous matches, or fewer courts with staggered rounds.
  • Mix Tournament (24 players): 6 courts.

Allow roughly 15 to 20 minutes per round, including changeovers. For a typical 6-round Americano with 8 players on 2 courts, you should book around two hours of court time. Add a buffer for late arrivals and any tie-breakers.

Step 4: Set Up the Tournament

This is where UberPadel saves you hours of work. Instead of manually drawing brackets, calculating fixtures, or building spreadsheets, you simply:

  1. Select your format on the UberPadel homepage.
  2. Enter the player names (and skill levels for the Mix format).
  3. Click start.

The system automatically generates every round of fixtures, assigning courts and ensuring balanced pairings. For Mexicano, fixtures update dynamically after each round. For Americano and Mix, the full schedule is generated upfront. No account is required and you can share the link with all participants.

Step 5: Run the Event

On the day, open the tournament page and manage each round:

  1. Announce the round: The fixture list shows who plays on which court.
  2. Play the round: Each match is played to the agreed point total (commonly 24 or 32 points).
  3. Enter the scores: Enter results directly on the tournament page from any device.
  4. Check the leaderboard: Scores update instantly so players can track their position in real time.
  5. Move to the next round: Once all matches are complete, proceed to the next fixtures.

This rhythm keeps the event running smoothly. Most groups complete a round every 15 to 20 minutes.

Step 6: Wrap Up and Share Results

Once the final round is done, the leaderboard shows the complete standings. The player (or team) with the most points wins. In the event of a tie, UberPadel uses head-to-head results and point difference as tiebreakers.

Share the tournament link with all participants so they can revisit stats and match history. Many organisers screenshot the final standings for the group chat to celebrate winners and build excitement for the next event.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced organisers can run into issues. Watch out for these common pitfalls:

  • Not booking enough courts: With 16 players and only 2 courts, half the group is always waiting. Stick to one court per four players.
  • Unbalanced skill groups: Inaccurate skill ratings lead to one-sided matches. Encourage honest self-assessment and adjust tiers after a warm-up round if needed.
  • Forgetting to track scores: With multiple courts playing at once it is easy to miss a result. Have players enter their own scores on the shared link after each match.
  • No time buffer: Players arrive late and changeovers take longer than expected. Add 20 to 30 minutes of buffer to your booking.
  • Overcomplicating the format: If it is your first tournament, start with Americano. It is the easiest to explain and everyone enjoys it.

With the right format, enough courts, and UberPadel to handle the logistics, you can focus on what matters most: great padel and a great time.
