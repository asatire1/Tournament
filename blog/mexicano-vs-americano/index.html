Mexicano vs Americano Padel: Which Tournament Format Should You Choose?

14 Feb 2025 Mexicano

If you have been around padel for any length of time, you have almost certainly heard of both Americano and Mexicano. They are easily the two most popular social tournament formats in the sport, and for good reason — they are fun, inclusive, and perfectly designed for groups of friends, club sessions, or one-off events. But they are not the same thing. Each serves a different purpose, and picking the right one can be the difference between a good session and a great one.

In this guide we break down exactly how each format works, compare them side by side, and help you decide which one fits your group best.

What is Americano?

Americano is the format most people start with. The setup is straightforward: before the tournament begins, the organiser generates a fixed schedule of rounds. In each round, players are paired into teams and assigned to courts. After every round the partnerships rotate, so by the end of the event everyone has played with — and against — as many different people as possible.

Points are tracked individually. You earn points based on the score your team achieves in each match, and those points accumulate across all rounds. At the end, the player with the highest total wins — regardless of how many matches their various teams won or lost.

The beauty of Americano lies in its simplicity. The schedule is decided up front, so everyone knows when they are playing and on which court. There are no surprises, no complicated bracket logic — just pick up your paddle and go. If you want a deeper dive into how it works, check out our full guide on What is Padel Americano?

What is Mexicano?

Mexicano takes the core idea of Americano — rotating partners, individual scoring — and adds a layer of dynamic matchmaking. Only the first round is randomly drawn. After that, the system looks at the current standings and pairs players so that those with similar point totals face each other in the next round.

This means the top players gradually play against tougher opposition, while those lower in the standings get more evenly matched games. The result is a self-balancing tournament where the final leaderboard reflects genuine performance rather than the luck of the draw. By the last few rounds, the matches at the top of the table are genuinely tight, and the overall winner has earned it.

Because the schedule cannot be planned in advance, Mexicano requires a bit more coordination between rounds — the organiser (or tool) needs to calculate standings and generate the next round on the fly. But that small overhead pays off in competitiveness and excitement.

When to Choose Americano

Americano shines when the priority is social — getting everyone playing together, keeping things relaxed, and making sure nobody feels out of their depth. It is the ideal pick for:

  • Social events and club nights — The fixed schedule means less downtime between rounds and no one waiting around while pairings are calculated.
  • Mixed-ability groups — Because partnerships rotate randomly, stronger players naturally carry weaker ones in some rounds and vice versa. This keeps things balanced without any explicit handicapping.
  • Newcomers — If half your group has never played a tournament before, Americano is the gentlest introduction. The rules are minimal and the atmosphere stays light.

When to Choose Mexicano

Mexicano is the better choice when your group cares about the final standings and wants every round to feel meaningful. Consider it for:

  • Regular playing groups — When the same people meet every week, Mexicano keeps things fresh because the matchups change based on form, not just a rotation.
  • Competitive players — The dynamic matchmaking ensures that by the later rounds the best players are facing each other. No one coasts to the title on easy draws.
  • Tighter final standings — Because similar-level players are matched together, point differences shrink and the leaderboard is more reflective of true ability. This makes the final result feel earned and fair.

Can You Run Both?

Absolutely. There is no rule that says you have to pick one format and stick with it forever. Many clubs alternate between the two depending on the occasion — Americano for casual weeknight sessions, Mexicano for the monthly competitive event. Some groups even start with Americano while players get used to the tournament concept, then graduate to Mexicano once everyone is comfortable.
